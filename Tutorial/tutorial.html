Chapter 01

Prepare your loom

A rigid-heddle loom keeps the warp in place and helps you form a clear opening for each pass of the weft.

01

Get familiar with the parts

The warp runs from the back beam to the front beam. The heddle holds the warp threads and moves between positions to create a shed. The shuttle carries the weft across the warp.

02

Gather your materials

Choose yarn suitable for your loom and heddle, and have a shuttle, scissors, and a measuring tape ready. Check your loom's instructions before dressing it.

Chapter 02

Plan your warp

The warp is the set of lengthwise threads held under tension on the loom. Planning its width and thread count gives your project a clear starting point.

Think about the finished cloth

Yarn thickness, spacing, and finishing affect the final width. Make a small sample when you are unsure, and follow the limits and guidance for your loom and yarn.

In the Heddle designer, Warp sets the number of threads across your draft and Weft sets its number of rows. The starting dimensions are 16 warp, 52 weft, and 3 mm thread width; change them to suit your plan.

Chapter 03

Design a pattern

Use a draft to explore how warp and weft colors meet before you start weaving.

  1. 01

    Open the designer and set the warp, weft, and thread width in Loom dimensions.

  2. 02

    Choose a color, then use the drawing tools to add it to the grid. Try a built-in pattern as a starting point.

  3. 03

    Adjust colors and repeats until you have a draft you like. Save your design so you can return to it later.

Keep your first draft simple

A few alternating colors or a plain weave repeat can make it easier to follow the pattern while you learn.

Chapter 04

Weave and finish

With the warp prepared and your draft nearby, work steadily and check the fabric as it grows.

01

Build a steady rhythm

Move the heddle to open a shed, pass the shuttle through, change the shed, and beat the weft into place. Keep an even, comfortable tension.

02

Check before finishing

Inspect the edges and cloth regularly. When the weaving is complete, secure the ends and finish the fabric using a method appropriate for your yarn.

You are ready to plan your next project.
